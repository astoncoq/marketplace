ACT I

The cost of standing aside

TODAY'S MODEL

You are notified after the relationship already happened.

Client
Discovers, subscribes, manages directly in the partner's world
direct relationship
Partner (Microsoft, Google, Palo Alto…)
Owns catalog, subscription, entitlement, support — on its own console
SIX STAGES HAPPEN OUTSIDE YOUR VIEW
Partner onboarding
Bilateral deal, negotiated by hand
Product onboarding
Catalog loaded manually, no sync
Fulfillment
Subscribed in partner's console
Invoicing
Monthly file, applied after the fact
Care
Triage only, no proactive view
Settlement
Reconciled from the same file
The operator's role: a monthly file drop, a reconciliation task, a support escalation. No visibility into the relationship while it's happening — only its paper trail afterward.
WHY IT BREAKS DOWN

Every big tech runs its own playbook. Today you integrate with each, one at a time.

Microsoft
Own catalog feed, CSP subscription model, its own lifecycle and billing cadence.
Google
Different marketplace semantics, different entitlement and usage-reporting model.
Palo Alto
Security-product provisioning and renewal logic, again its own shape entirely.
Every new partner today means a new bespoke integration into your CRM B2B and marketplace. That cost compounds — and it's the reason expansion stalls.
THE BUSINESS COST

Standing aside isn't neutral — it has a price.

No visibility
Your own sales and care teams can't see what a client actually has active with a partner today.
No commercial leverage
You can't bundle, cross-sell or renegotiate terms on a relationship you don't own operationally.
Brand exposure
When something breaks on the partner's console, the client still calls you first — with none of your tools to fix it.
A core stack under strain
Every partner's idiosyncrasies land directly on the CRM B2B and marketplace — coupling grows with every deal.
ACT II

The strategic shift

THE SHIFT

Move from the sidelines to active intermediary.

TODAY
Client ↔ Partner
Telco is a billing pass-through, informed after the fact.
→
TOMORROW
Client ↔ Telco ↔ Partner
Telco owns onboarding, entitlement, invoicing and care — orchestrating every partner behind one experience.
This is not "integrate with more partners." It's taking back the middle of the relationship — so every future partner is a configuration, not a project.
INSIDE THE PIL

Four jobs, done once, for every partner.

Catalog ingestion
Pulls each partner's native catalog and maps it to one internal product model.
TMF620
Order & subscription orchestration
Translates a single client order into whatever the partner's API or console expects.
TMF622
Entitlement & lifecycle events
Normalizes activation, change and cancellation events back into one client inventory.
TMF621 / TMF676
Usage & settlement normalization
Converts partner billing files/APIs into one revenue-share and invoicing format.
TMF678 / TMF632·651
WHY THIS PROTECTS YOU

Your CRM B2B and marketplace should never touch a partner directly again.

Stability
A new partner, or a partner API change, is absorbed in the PIL — the core stack never redeploys for it.
Speed
Onboarding partner #4 is a connector, not a program — because #1 through #3 already proved the pattern.
Control
Standards-based (TM Forum) internal contract means governance and reporting are consistent, partner by partner.

ACT IV

Getting there

HOW WE GET THERE

Prove control with one partner before scaling to all of them.

PHASE 0
Stand up the PIL
One partner connector (e.g. Microsoft), proving orchestration end-to-end without touching the core stack.
PHASE 1
Partner Hub
Self-serve onboarding and catalog sync — new partners plug in without an IT project.
PHASE 2
Open Shopfront
Launch as the governed channel for agencies and third-party sales teams.
PHASE 3
Full scale
Customer 360 and automated settlement across every partner and channel.
BEYOND TECHNOLOGY

This is an operating-model change, not just a system.

A partner-ops owner
One team owns the PIL's rules and every partner connector — replacing ad hoc bilateral deals.
Sales & care enablement
Direct and agency teams work from the same live inventory and entitlement view.
Governance, not tickets
New partner terms are a workflow approval, not an IT backlog item.
THE DECISION

Pick one partner. Prove the model. Then scale it to every partner and channel.

1. Choose the pilot partner
Likely Microsoft, given existing volume and process maturity.
2. Scope the first flow
End-to-end for one product line: onboarding through settlement.
3. Set the proof window
A defined timeframe to validate control before wider rollout.
